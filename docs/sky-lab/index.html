Sky Lab tutorial

Tune a track's sky, fog and grade, the weather presets, and how rain, snow and grit look, live on the real track, then save straight into the files the game loads.

What it is

Sky Lab is a dev scene for Full Throttle Flux, the companion to Surface Lab. It runs a real circuit with the game's own lighting and weather code, with an editor on top, so every change draws the way a race draws it.

It covers four layers that only look right together:

  • The sky: the track's sun, sky colours, clouds, god rays and night sky.
  • Post: the track's fog, glow, ambient occlusion and grade.
  • Weather: the 13 presets, from clear to a heavy dust storm, and what each does to the road and the sky.
  • Falling: how rain, snow and dust-storm grit look.
The weather is laid on top of the track's own sky, as it is in a race. The lab keeps the two apart, so previewing a storm never ends up in the sky you save.

Launching it

In the Godot editor, open res://tools/sky_lab/sky_lab.tscn and press F6. Or from the repo root:

godot --path full_throttle_flux res://tools/sky_lab/sky_lab.tscn

It opens on Utopia in clear weather, with the Glide camera paused on the track. To open Swoopy or Hairpin Alley instead, set LAB_TRACK to test_circuit_5_live or test_circuit_6_live.

The screen

  1. Top bar: camera, look, weather, compare, save.
  2. 3D view: the real track, drawn the way the game draws it.
  3. Tabs: Sky, Weather, Falling, Post and Track.
  4. Checks: problems found as you work.
  5. Sun dial: where the sun is; drag it.
  6. Exposure: how bright the frame is.
  7. Readout: what the weather is doing right now.

Your first look

A five-minute run through the main idea, with nothing saved until the last step.

  1. On the sun dial, drag the sun toward the horizon and watch the light turn golden.
  2. Choose Look: toward sun to see the sky around it.
  3. In the Sky tab, raise coverage under Clouds.
  4. Pick Rain medium from the weather menu. Coverage now reads weather sets it: rain brings its own clouds.
  5. Hold C to see the saved look in the same rain, and let go to return to yours.
  6. Press Ctrl+Z until you're back where you started, or press Ctrl+S to review what would be saved.

Looking around

A sky is judged from more than the driving line. The Look menu keeps the Glide camera's position and turns it, so you can check the sun's glare, the far side of the sky and the clouds overhead without leaving the spot you're tuning.

Readouts

The exposure readout reads the frame back from the GPU twice a second, which costs a little. Untick Measure exposure when you're reading the GPU time.

Sky tab

Everything on the track's sky controller, grouped as in its Inspector. It's saved to the track's lighting scene, so it reaches this track only.

GroupWhat it controls
SunWhere it is (azimuth, elevation), how strong, and a tint over the colour its height gives it.
AtmosphereZenith, horizon and fog colours, distance fog, ambient light and exposure.
CloudsCoverage, density, absorption, deck height and thickness, cirrus, wind, billow size and detail, and how much cover dims the sun.
God raysThe screen-space rays, and volumetric shafts (costly).
NightStars and city glow.
Cloud qualityMarch steps and dither. Ultra is 64 steps.

What the weather changes

Each row's badge says how far it reaches. Under a weather that changes a value, the badge says so instead, in orange. An edit there still saves, but you'll only see it in weathers that leave the value alone.

BadgeMeans
weather sets itReplaced outright, from the weather's sky preset or its own override.
weather blends 60%Mixed toward the weather's sky preset by its sky_mood.
weather tints itThe fog colour, pushed toward the weather's fog_tint.
weather x 0.60The sun's energy, scaled by sun_scale.

Sun position, the sky's colours (unless blended), exposure and cloud quality always stay the track's own, so tune those in any weather.

Example: a lower sun

sun_elevation from 23° to 7°, and sun_azimuth swung round to 250°. The light colour follows the sun's height by itself.

Example: heavier clouds

coverage to 0.78, billow_size to 1.8 and a darker cloud_shadow_color.

Post tab

The track's Environment, minus what the sky controller drives. It's saved to the same lighting scene as the sky.

GroupWhat it controls
Distance fogHow much the sun scatters into it, aerial perspective, and how much it covers the sky. Its colour and density are on the Sky tab.
Height fogA layer of fog near the road. Fog and storm weathers set it.
TonemapThe white point.
GlowBloom from lights, signs and bright sky.
Ambient occlusionSSAO and SSIL.
GradingBrightness, contrast and saturation.

Example: haze

More height fog and aerial perspective, with denser distance fog from the Sky tab. The city recedes and the road stays clear.

Example: grade

Contrast 1.25, saturation 1.35 and more glow. Keep an eye on the exposure readout's clipped figure as you push contrast.

Weather tab

The weather chosen in the top bar. Each is a file in resources/weather, so an edit reaches every track that uses it.

GroupWhat it controls
GeneralName, kind (clear, rain, snow, fog, dust storm) and intensity.
Surface targetsHow wet, frozen or dusty the road gets, humidity, how much falls, and how long the road takes to change mid-race.
WindGround wind (slants rain, drifts dust) and an optional direction.
SkyA sky preset to borrow clouds from, cover, fog densities, how much of the preset's mood to take, fog tint, sun scale, height fog, and the glow on the road's lines in poor visibility. Negative means "keep the track's own".

Weather is visual only. Nothing here changes handling, so trained AI lines and recordings stay valid.

Falling tab

How the current weather's kind looks as it falls. There's one look per kind, shared by light, medium and heavy and by every track: the weather's precipitation sets how much falls. Clear and fog have nothing to edit.

GroupWhat it controls
FallSpeed, how much the wind carries it, spread, sway for flakes, and how much of it shows.
Near streaksThe particles round the camera: width, length range, the "shutter" that sets how long a streak is at speed, roundness, opacity.
Far layerThe pattern on a cylinder further out, for depth.
ColourThe track's fog colour pushed toward a target colour, or the track's dust colour for storms.

Saved to resources/weather/precip. These files hold exactly the look the game had before, so nothing changes until you edit one.

Example: heavier rain

Rain's streaks made wider, longer and more opaque. Streaks stretch with the camera's own speed, so judge this in Chase lap too.

Example: snow

Bigger flakes with more sway, and a pink target colour.

Track tab

The track's own environment, where it has one: how dusty its road is in dry weather, and the colour of its dust, used by dust trails, the dusty road and dust storms. Saved to the track's TrackEnvironment file.

Which weathers a track allows, and the one a race starts in, also live there, and are edited in its Inspector for now.

Checks

The list under the tabs updates when you pause. Double-click an item to switch to that weather.

It looks forWhy
A sky preset that doesn't existThe game would warn and ignore it.
sky_mood with no sky presetThere's nothing to blend toward.
Precipitation that does nothing, or nothing fallingOnly rain, snow and dust storms fall.
Distance fog 90% within 400 mThe road ahead disappears fast at racing speed. Fine for a heavy fog, if it's meant.
A look's max length under its minStreaks can't stretch.
Volumetric shafts, ultra cloudsThe two costliest settings: check the GPU time.

Compare, snapshots and undo

  • Hold C (or the button) to see the saved look, in the weather you're in. Let go to return to your edits.
  • Snapshots A and B: click an empty button to store the current look, then click it to return there. Ctrl+click stores over it.
  • Undo and redo: Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y cover every edit, across all tabs. A slider drag, or a whole Start from, counts as one step.

Saving

Save or Ctrl+S lists the files first, and nothing is written until you confirm.

Edit inSaved toReaches
Sky, PostThe track's lighting scene, such as scenes/tracks/utopia/utopia_lighting.tscnThis track
Trackresources/track_fx/This track
Weatherresources/weather/Every track using that weather
Fallingresources/weather/precip/Every weather of that kind, on every track

Saves change only the lines of the values you edited, so they make small, readable diffs, and git is your safety net. Previewing a weather is never saved: the sky you save is the track's own.

Saving needs an editor build of Godot. If the editor has a saved file open, it may ask to reload it.

Limits

  • Tracks with a sky rig. Utopia, Swoopy and Hairpin Alley have one. Builder tracks use a plain sky, so the Sky tab is empty there and weather can't change their sky.
  • Named sky presets are read-only. Start from one, then save the result to the track.
  • No new weathers. Edit the thirteen there are. Which weathers a track allows stays in its environment file.
  • One falling look per kind. Light and heavy rain share one; the amount comes from the weather.
